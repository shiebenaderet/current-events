Sixty Learning Targets

One target for every substantive section across the eight topic pages. These are a draft written from what each section already teaches — please correct them. A wrong target is worse than none, because it propagates into the revision of that whole section.

They also unlock the check the site has never run. Q1, the passage-independence probe, hands a separate model a target and that section's quiz questions, withholds the section itself, and counts what it can answer anyway. Anything it gets right never required reading the page. There are 76 quizzes here and none has been tested that way.

Rule 1 — it survives the news

A target built on the 2026 midterms expires on November 4. One built on how a primary can end a career before the general election does not. Volatile facts belong in the dated update card; the target holds what stays true.

Rule 2 — it names a relation, not a topic

The Holodomor is a topic. A famine can be engineered, and sealed borders are how you tell is a target — it says what a student should be able to do with the section, which is the thing a quiz can actually test.

Artificial Intelligence

ai.html 10 targets

  1. What AI actually is

    Most AI does not reason from rules a person wrote; it finds patterns in large amounts of data, which is a different kind of machine from a normal program

  2. How a machine learns

    A system can learn a category from labelled examples and its own corrected mistakes, without anyone stating the rule that defines the category

  3. What made AI powerful

    Neural networks were not a new idea in 2012 — data, computing power and technique arrived together, and that is what changed

  4. What AI can do now

    The change since 2022 is not that chatbots got smarter but that systems began taking multi-step actions on their own

  5. AI and your school

    Reading and thinking are skills built by doing them, so a tool that does them for you skips the part that was building the skill

  6. Will AI take jobs?

    A survey measures what people expect, which is evidence about people and not about the future

  7. Talking to animals

    Finding structure in a signal comes before understanding it — nobody is translating whale song, and pattern-finding is the step that must come first

  8. The godfather's warning

    A person can build a technology for forty years and still judge it dangerous; expertise and alarm are not opposites

  9. AI that takes action

    An agent that carries out a whole task fails differently from a tool that answers one question

  10. AI through the decades

    AI's history runs in long stalls and sudden jumps, and the jumps came when a bottleneck was removed, not when someone had a new idea

Climate Change

climate-change.html 7 targets

  1. Where Things Stand

    A single hot summer is weather; what makes a heat wave evidence is where it sits in a long record

  2. Deep-Time Climate History

    Scientists can measure a climate nobody recorded, because natural materials keep datable evidence of the air around them

  3. The Greenhouse Effect

    The greenhouse effect is why Earth is habitable; the problem is a change in degree, not the existence of the mechanism

  4. What's Different This Time

    The rate of change is the finding — CO₂ has been higher before, but it moved over millennia, not decades

  5. Effects Being Observed

    Global averages and local evidence are two different kinds of measurement, and Rainier's glaciers are the local kind

  6. Washington's Climate Story

    A state can price carbon, and the argument about what that costs at the pump is a real dispute with more than one honest number in it

  7. How Did We Learn All This?

    This science is nearly two centuries old, and Eunice Foote's 1856 experiment measured the effect before anyone had a theory for it

Gun Violence

gun-violence.html 8 targets

  1. Where things stand

    Different organizations count school shootings differently, so two true totals for the same year can disagree

  2. 1 · Understanding the scale

    Most U.S. gun deaths are suicides, and a rate per 100,000 and a raw count rank the fifty states differently

  3. 2 · The Second Amendment

    *Heller* (2008) changed the legal question from militia service to an individual right, and courts read the same sentence differently before and after

  4. 3 · Safety measures tried

    A measure can be popular, visible and widely adopted and still lack evidence that it prevents what it was adopted to prevent

  5. 4 · How policy is built

    Federal law sets a floor and states build above it, which is why the rules change when you cross a state line

  6. Washington's story

    Washington's extreme risk protection order was written by voters at the ballot box, not by the legislature

  7. How other countries handle this

    Australia and Switzerland reached very different gun policies from very different histories, and neither transfers whole to the U.S.

  8. History timeline

    Major federal gun laws follow specific events, and the pattern of which events produced law is itself the thing to notice

Immigration

immigration.html 8 targets

  1. Where things stand

    Most green cards go through family sponsorship, and the queue's length is a fact about the system's design rather than about any applicant

  2. Colonial era

    Forced migration is not immigration, and the distinction changes what the rest of the story means

  3. The great waves

    People leave when staying becomes impossible; the Irish famine shows a push cause, not a pull one

  4. The quota era

    The 1924 Act chose the 1890 census on purpose, and choosing which year to count is how a law picks who may come

  5. The modern system begins

    The 1965 Act replaced national-origin quotas with family and skill preferences, and that swap reshaped who immigrates

  6. How the system works

    Two true facts sit together: the U.S. admits over a million people a year, and some queues run for decades

  7. Washington's story

    Immigration built this state's specific towns and industries, and the Scandinavian wave is the local case

  8. History timeline

    U.S. immigration law has swung open and shut repeatedly, and each swing names the group it was reacting to
