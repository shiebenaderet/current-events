# Learning targets — one per substantive section

**Status: draft, awaiting teacher review.** Nothing downstream should treat
these as settled until that review happens.

## Why this file exists

`reading-intervention`'s G3 gate refuses to run three checks without a
declared learning target, and the most valuable of them is **Q1, the
passage-independence probe**: give a separate model call the quiz questions
and the target, withhold the section, and count how many questions it can
answer anyway. Any question it gets right is a question that never required
reading the section.

Q1 is the check that decides whether the other work mattered. A section can
be cohesive, glossed, correctly sourced and beautifully paced, and if its
quiz is answerable without it, all of that was spent on a page students can
skip. There are 76 quizzes on this site and **not one has been tested this
way**, because until now there were no targets to test them against.

The targets have a second job. Every "Where things stand" section is
volatile by construction — it expires on a date. A durable target is what
those sections can be rebuilt around when the facts move, so that refreshing
one is a matter of changing what is true, not rediscovering what the section
was for.

## How these were written

From what each section already teaches, not from its title, and not from
what would be convenient to test. Two rules:

- **It survives the news.** "The 2026 midterms" expires on November 4.
  "How a primary can end a career before the general election" does not.
- **It names a relation, not a topic.** "The Holodomor" is a topic. "A
  famine can be engineered, and sealed borders are how you tell" is a target
  — it says what a student should be able to *do* with the section.

## ai.html

| Section | Target |
|---|---|
| What AI actually is | Most AI does not reason from rules a person wrote; it finds patterns in large amounts of data, which is a different kind of machine from a normal program |
| How a machine learns | A system can learn a category from labelled examples and its own corrected mistakes, without anyone stating the rule that defines the category |
| What made AI powerful | Neural networks were not a new idea in 2012 — data, computing power and technique arrived together, and that is what changed |
| What AI can do now | The change since 2022 is not that chatbots got smarter but that systems began taking multi-step actions on their own |
| AI and your school | Reading and thinking are skills built by doing them, so a tool that does them for you skips the part that was building the skill |
| Will AI take jobs? | A survey measures what people expect, which is evidence about people and not about the future |
| Talking to animals | Finding structure in a signal comes before understanding it — nobody is translating whale song, and pattern-finding is the step that must come first |
| The godfather's warning | A person can build a technology for forty years and still judge it dangerous; expertise and alarm are not opposites |
| AI that takes action | An agent that carries out a whole task fails differently from a tool that answers one question |
| AI through the decades | AI's history runs in long stalls and sudden jumps, and the jumps came when a bottleneck was removed, not when someone had a new idea |

## What happens next

1. Teacher reviews and corrects. A wrong target is worse than none — it
   propagates into the revision of the whole section.
2. Q1 runs per page: a separate agent gets the target and that page's quiz
   questions, never the section text, and answers what it can.
3. Every question it answers correctly is reported, and the fix is a better
   question, never an easier one.
